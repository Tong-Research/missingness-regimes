# MIMIC-IV — the regime the method targets — pre-registration

## Why this dataset, and why now

Paper 1's premise is that a clinician's decision not to order a test carries information about the
patient. PLCO cannot test that premise well: its missingness is generated by the trial *protocol* —
screening arm and visit attendance — and its mask informativeness is $\iota = 0.12$–$0.15$. Paper 3's
eleven public datasets were chosen for *native* missingness, not informative missingness. So the regime
the method was built for has not yet been tried on real data.

MIMIC-IV is that regime. Features are the first-24-hour means of 42 frequent ICU labs; the outcome is
in-hospital mortality; the missingness is *which labs were ordered*. The labs whose absence most changes
the outcome are the liver panel and the blood gas: mortality $6\%$ when unordered against $17\%$ when
ordered. The sick get more tests. Measured with the article's own `mask_informativeness` (5-fold, seed 0):

| | $\iota$ (AUROC $- 0.5$) | $H_{\mathrm{exc}}$ (5 permutation seeds) |
|---|---|---|
| PLCO, best cohort (Colorectal) | $0.151$ | — |
| Synthetic suite, 40 configurations where the method wins (mean) | — | $+0.478$ |
| Synthetic suite, the other 68 (mean) | — | $+0.041$ |
| **MIMIC-IV, full $n = 66{,}989$** | **$0.218$** | **$+0.306 \pm 0.053$** |
| MIMIC-IV, 40,000-record subsample, seed 0 | $0.227$ | $+0.104 \pm 0.074$ |

Two diagnostics, and they say different things. $\iota$ measures the premise and is well above PLCO.
$H_{\mathrm{exc}}$ — the excess heterogeneity of the per-pattern coefficients over their mask-permuted
value, the one diagnostic that ranks the synthetic benefit (Spearman $+0.51$, AUC $0.757$;
`results/diagnostic_validation.csv`, recomputed today) — puts the full cohort between the synthetic
non-winners and winners, and the 40,000 subsample close to the non-winners. So the article's own
predictor says: **a modest gain at full $n$, and probably none on the PLCO-sized subsample.** That is
why the run uses the full cohort, and why prediction 2 below is calibrated to modest.

**This is a single, pre-registered test.** If the method has a home on real data, it is here. If it
loses here, the negative result is complete across both missingness regimes and Paper 3's mechanism —
coefficient heterogeneity is not predictive heterogeneity — generalises to the hardest case.
No further datasets will be added after this one; that is the discipline that keeps it honest.

## Data

MIMIC-IV 3.0 via `experiments/mimic4_sites.py`, unchanged: first ICU stay per admission, adults,
$\ge 24$ h; $n = 66,989$, $d = 42$, prevalence $10.98%$, $23.6%$ missing, 2,029 distinct
patterns, 202 with support $\ge 30$. Held under a data use agreement; per-decision outputs are not
released. **The full $n = 66{,}989$ is used**, not the PLCO 40,000 subsample: the article's mechanism is
rows per pattern, and $H_{\mathrm{exc}}$ on the subsample already predicts no gain. 10 seeds $\times$ 5
stratified folds as in the main table.

## Arms, all sharing $(\text{seed}, \text{fold})$

The seven main-table methods, frozen and at their published operating points: `mean_impute`,
`mean_indicator`, `mask_interaction`, `histgb_native`, `pattern_submodels`, `spsm_global`, `ours`
($\kappa$ = median training-fold support). Plus one candidate: `rooted_cv` (R1, the hierarchy re-rooted
at the imputed model, $\kappa$ by inner CV), included on its construction and not on its PLCO result,
which is unscored at the time of writing. Every comparator's regularisation is tuned inside the training
folds. The published permuted-mask null (`oracle_ceiling.permute_mask`) is run for `ours` and
`rooted_cv`.

## Predictions

1. **Regime confirmed:** $\iota \ge 0.18$ and $H_{\mathrm{exc}} \ge 0.15$ on the training folds. Both
   measured before registering ($0.218$, $+0.306$); this is a guard, not a finding.
2. **`ours` beats `mean_impute` by $\ge 0.002$ paired**, $p < 0.05$, **and by less than $0.02$.** The
   mask carries $\iota \approx 0.22$ of signal that mean imputation discards; $H_{\mathrm{exc}} = +0.31$
   says the patterns differ in what they predict, but less than the synthetic winners do. Modest is the
   calibrated expectation, and a gain above $0.02$ would itself need explaining.
3. **`ours` does NOT beat `mean_indicator` by the floor.** The indicator captures lab-ordering linearly;
   the hierarchy's row-restricted fits still pay for fragmentation. This is the honest expectation, and
   it is the prediction I would most like to be wrong.
4. **`ours` beats its own permuted null by $\ge 0.002$.** This would be the first dataset in the project
   where real structure exceeds its null — the regime test in one line.
5. **Within family, `ours` beats `spsm_global` and `pattern_submodels`**, as on all four PLCO cohorts.
6. **`rooted_cv` $\ge$ `mean_impute`** by construction, **and selects an interior $\kappa$** in the
   majority of folds, because here there is something to find.

## Withdrawal conditions

- **(a)** $\iota < 0.15$ on the subsample: the regime premise fails; stop, report nothing.
- **(b)** `ours` below `mean_impute` by $\ge 0.002$: the method loses in its own regime. Report it as the
  decisive negative it is; it ends the search for a home.
- **(c)** Real $\le$ null for `ours`: the mask's signal is not pattern-structured even here; Paper 3's
  mechanism holds in the hardest case. Report.
- **(d)** Any within-family loss contradicts four PLCO cohorts: bug hunt before reporting anything.
- **(e)** Prediction 3 fails — `ours` beats `mean_indicator` by the floor — is the outcome that would
  change Paper 1 most. It must replicate on a second seed block (seeds 10–19) before any sentence in
  the article changes.

## What each outcome does to Paper 1

- 2 and 4 hold, 3 fails: a positive real-data claim in the regime $\iota$ identifies; the article's story
  becomes *when, and how to tell*, with $\iota$ as the instrument.
- 2 and 4 hold, 3 holds: the pattern is worth modelling here and the cheapest way to model it wins.
- 2 fails: the negative result is complete across both regimes.
